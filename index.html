Professional Self-Assessment

I am a Computer Science student completing my degree with a concentration in data analytics, and this capstone represents the culmination of twenty-two courses across the program. Alongside my coursework, I work at IBM in a role focused on data validation and approval workflows for engineering, supplier, and finance data, and I co-own Black Squirrel Sealcoating LLC, a small business I operate part-time. My long-term goal is to eventually transition from my current role at IBM to launch an analytics and management consulting firm serving small trades businesses, an industry where my working career began before I moved into data analytics. This program, and the process of building this ePortfolio in particular, has sharpened both the technical skills and the professional habits I intend to carry into that next stage of my career.

Collaboration and Stakeholder Communication

Collaboration and stakeholder communication have been a consistent thread throughout both my academic and professional work, even though the majority of my program coursework was completed on an individual basis. At IBM, I recently assumed a co-team lead role alongside two colleagues, one of whom is based in Singapore, requiring our team to work effectively across significant time zone differences to remain aligned. In my daily responsibilities, I coordinate engineering change content across teams and departments worldwide, ensuring that information is accurate and released on schedule, as errors at this stage can directly impact manufacturing operations. I have also collaborated with colleagues at IBM on a patented internal process, and am listed as co-inventor on U.S. Patent 12,436,874, Computer System Test Parallelization, which required working closely with other engineers to develop and formally document an approach for review and protection. Within the program itself, collaboration took a more informal form through discussion boards, where students worked through project questions together across several courses.

Data Structures, Algorithms, Software Engineering, and Databases

Data structures and algorithms, along with software engineering and database work, are the technical core of what this program and this capstone have demonstrated. Beyond the three artifacts in this ePortfolio, I worked on a project in a data analytics course that involved merging and analyzing real crime and storm event datasets using Python, MySQL, and Jupyter Notebooks, which required the same kind of structural thinking about data organization and query efficiency that shows up in my enhanced database artifact here. My capstone enhancements themselves reflect direct, deliberate work in this space, including refactoring a contact management system from a linear data structure to a hash-based one for efficiency, and building a MongoDB aggregation pipeline to produce grouped, summarized data rather than only flat query results.

Security

Security has become an increasingly central part of how I approach my technical work, both in this program and at IBM, where data validation and approval workflows exist specifically to catch errors and vulnerabilities before they reach production systems. In this capstone, that mindset is most visible in my database enhancement, where I identified that a reusable module had been built securely, accepting credentials as parameters rather than hardcoding them, while the application built on top of it hadn't followed that same discipline. Closing that gap, along with improving how failed database connections are handled, was one of the clearest, most concrete demonstrations of a security mindset across my entire capstone.

How the Artifacts Fit Together

The three artifacts that follow in this ePortfolio were chosen specifically to represent growth across the three key areas of computer science this program covers. My software design and engineering artifact, a contact management system originally built in CS 320, demonstrates data structure selection, defensive programming, and test coverage design. My algorithms and data structures artifact, a reinforcement learning agent originally built in CS 370, demonstrates algorithm evaluation and experiment design through added visualization and comparison tooling. My databases artifact, a MongoDB dashboard originally built in CS 340, demonstrates secure configuration, query design, and defensive error handling. Together, these three pieces of work reflect both where I started in this program and the more deliberate, security-conscious engineer I've become by the end of it.
